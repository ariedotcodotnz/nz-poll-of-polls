How well has the model done in the past?

Each variant was refitted using only the polls available 26, 8, 4 and 1 weeks before the 2017, 2020 and 2023 elections (a poll counts once its fieldwork ended and it would typically have been published) and scored against the official result. CRPS is the average error of the whole forecast distribution in percentage points (lower is better); 90% coverage is the share of parties whose result fell inside the 90% interval and should be close to 0.9. The published ensemble's weights for each election were fitted on the other two elections only, so its scores are out of sample.

About the model

Latent weekly vote intention for {{ parties|length - 1 }} parties (plus "other") follows a correlated random walk on the log-ratio scale, pinned to the official results of the {{ anchor_year }}–{{ last_result_year }} elections. Each poll is modelled around the latent state plus a house effect (a persistent pollster bias and a per-term deviation) and an industry-wide polling error that drifts from its value after one election to its value at the next. The 2026 election-day polling error cannot be seen yet, so it is drawn from the errors of past elections, which widens the forecast. Each pollster's effective sample size is learned from the data, unreported parties are treated as unobserved rather than zero, and support moves faster in the final eight weeks of a campaign. Model variants are combined with weights from rolling-origin backtests. Seats use the Sainte-Laguë formula with the 5% threshold, electorate lifeline, overhang and independent-seat rules.
